# AZMARA game concept and thematic direction

Updated: 11 October 2026. Phase: desktop/web visual iteration and rule definition.
Authority: the Founder's setting and theme update (`DEC-008`), provisional
name (`DEC-009`), platform direction (`DEC-010`), fictional world (`DEC-011`)
and town design scope, reference direction and rules discussion
(`DEC-012` through `DEC-065`) in the
[approved decisions](approved/DECISIONS.md).

The provisional game name is **AZMARA**, chosen by the Founder for now. Final
branding and name clearance remain unresolved. The provisional-name decision
alone does not rename the existing project directory or authorise external
registrations. The later purchased-domain connection is approved under `DEC-041`.

The game is an original, mobile-first, persistent online multiplayer strategy
game. Its primary historical and visual inspiration is the Abbasid world during
the golden age of Abbasid Baghdad, particularly the city
at its height. The Founder has selected a fictional inspired world and a
player-owned town. Detailed geography, lore and gameplay systems remain open.

The aim is a distinct civilisation-building experience whose setting informs
its eventual design, not an Ancient Greek strategy game with renamed buildings
and different artwork. A first local visual design is now authorised; the
production game, engine and online systems are not yet specified.

Under `DEC-048`, the Founder wants the eventual game heavily concentrated on
trade, war and economics. The original building catalogue supplies a first
visual set of 30 active types for review, not a completed production economy.
A 31-entry compatibility registry retains the retired Shipyard ID for existing
saved buildings and queues; it is not offered for a new build. Workshop already
covers equipment, so no duplicate Siege Works is added (`DEC-061`).
The Town adviser is labelled “Advisor”; Town navigation and Town Hall retain
their existing names. Working building names and rules will evolve together.

`DEC-049` separates the private Dev workbench from the player-facing Gameplay
presentation. Coloured controls sit before Preview/Help/Options on desktop;
compact browsers offer them in the existing menu. Dev retains design notes,
the artwork catalogue and local demonstration/reset tools. Gameplay hides those
tests and retains town information and normal navigation. Each reload starts
in Dev for this owner-private workbench. Both modes use the same saved town;
the transient switch changes no resources, plots or valid construction timer.
Gameplay remains an incomplete visual prototype, not production gameplay or
an admin/security boundary. Further testing functions remain to be designed.

## Target platforms

browser game, not only a promotional website. The original mobile-first
direction remains in force; browser layouts and controls also need consideration.

These targets do not select an engine, client architecture, shared-account
model, backend, supported device versions or simultaneous launch date. A shared
world and progress across devices is a proposal to evaluate, not a requirement
silently added by the platform decision.

## Primary inspiration

The Founder has selected Abbasid-era Baghdad and its golden age as
the main sources of inspiration. Areas to explore include:

- Great cities, settlements, different regions and peoples, architecture and
  craftsmanship.
- Merchant networks, bazaars, marketplaces, caravan routes and long-distance
  commerce.
- Agriculture and the infrastructure that supports settlements.
- Scholarship, libraries, astronomy, mathematics, medicine, engineering and
  cultural and technological advancement.
- Diplomacy, armies, military development and territorial expansion.

These are themes for future investigation, not claims that every institution
or technology belongs to the same place and moment. Specific historical
details require research before they are presented as fact.

## Fictional inspired world

Under `DEC-011`, AZMARA uses original fictional geography, cities and characters
inspired by Abbasid civilisation and the golden age of Abbasid Baghdad. It is not a strict
historical simulator or a claim to reproduce actual Baghdad. Real historical
details still require research when presented as fact.

Exact world geography, chronology, political structures and lore remain open.
Baghdad is a primary historical reference, not an approved playable capital.

`DEC-030` approves two-axis finger exploration and plus/minus map zoom for
this prototype. Camera scale is an interface setting, not town progression
or a new economic rule. Keep artwork and building hit areas registered and

The Founder likes the first visual draft and wants the underlying town-owning
strategy experience understood deeply (`DEC-014`). The reference goal is a
connected economy in a persistent world: people, income, production, buildings,
knowledge, transport and neighbours should make decisions consequential. It is
not simply a sequence of upgrade timers. The relationships and a proposed
first design slice are in the [reference research](ideas/IKARIAM_RESEARCH.md).
Individual rules, resources, quantities and features still need their own design.

## Population capacity and satisfaction

Under `DEC-025`, the town's main civic building sets its maximum population.
Upgrading it provides more space but does not instantly add citizens.
"Town Hall" is its working name, not a final building-name selection.
`DEC-032` also selects that label and level-1 starting state for the new visual
layout, replacing the illustrative Council House presentation only.

Satisfaction determines population growth within that capacity. Grain supports
satisfaction through the civic provision service already agreed under
`DEC-016` and `DEC-019`. Housing capacity and satisfaction are separate
requirements; extra space alone does not guarantee more people.

Under `DEC-026`, civic provision needs to keep pace with a developing town.
Inadequate satisfaction slows population growth; sufficiently low satisfaction
makes residents leave. Increasing housing capacity alone does not ensure that
the current food service can support the larger town. Grain takes the reference
wine role; the Founder's example quantities and percentages are not balance values.

Under `DEC-027`, provision demand follows actual residents rather than Town
Hall level alone. More residents require more provision to sustain the same
satisfaction. Increasing empty housing capacity does not itself increase
resident demand or immediately reduce satisfaction, with other conditions
unchanged. Grain supports growth through satisfaction; it does not directly
create citizens or bypass the housing limit.

Capacity by level, demand quantities, service limits, consumption, growth and
departure rates, satisfaction thresholds, baseline and other satisfaction
sources remain open. Departures are a satisfaction consequence,
not an approved instant population loss whenever grain runs out. Allocation
handling when population falls also needs a rule. The main civic building's
level does not yet define the production town-level model. `DEC-053` separately
uses completed Hall levels for web prototype artwork milestones. Wine is not an
AZMARA resource. `DEC-048` includes Museum as a visual
building design, while cultural exchanges and satisfaction effects remain open.

## Current geography first draft

DEC-057/058/061 define **Town → Region → World**. A Region is a shared geographic
area larger than one town, not a city, country or a political owner. It can
eventually host independent players' towns; live ownership/multiplayer remains
unimplemented. The fictional setting uses contiguous inland desert basins,
dunes, ridges and overland routes rather than islands or river corridors.
Town, Region and World are land-only: no navigable water, actual rivers, sea,
boats or naval combat. Tiny decorative oasis pools and fountains are allowed,
without transport, irrigation or economic effects. Earlier water/coastal art
remains historical rather than being overwritten.

This first draft preserves the 23 town plot identities, saved levels and queues,
resource identities, and 16 ordinary regional settlement locations plus a
locked premium preview. Regional land anchors change to suit the new painting;
none creates a colony, spends resources or grants premium ownership. Stable
internal legacy island IDs remain only for compatibility. Visible navigation
and descriptions use Region; Route Archive is the current navigation-building
label. Prior island/coastal descriptions in this document are historical.

DEC-058 fills the full Region/World viewport with original desert scenery;
outer scenery does not introduce extra playable sites. World is finite,
scrollable/drag/touch/keyboard navigable coordinate geography, not a card gallery.
Its twelve fictional Regions retain their existing coordinates and stable IDs.
Compact overview may use bounded panning to keep actual 44px targets distinct.

DEC-061 sets the qualitative overland transport direction: Pack Caravans carry
smaller loads faster; Heavy Wagon Convoys carry bulk more slowly. Caravan Station
and Freight Depot are the active transport-building names. Their legacy IDs
and reserved roles remain for valid towns/queues. Capacities, speeds, upkeep,
prices, routes, army composition and economic balance still need design; the
prototype does not move goods or units between towns.

Generated bare/developed town terrain and original cosmetic intermediate
ground stages preserve Hall-led visual growth without adding infrastructure
mechanics. The all-building level-50 Dev work continues alongside this art pass.
Native remains paused; the web draft stays owner-private.

## Shared regional resource model and navigation

DEC-057 renames the shared geography, not the previously approved resource
relationships below. The working resource model has four regional variants:
timber with clay, timber with silver, timber with grain, or timber with iron.
A town sits within a Region; its buildings are not the Region's entire
production infrastructure. The
number of production sites and detailed production rules remain open. Under
`DEC-021`, towns in the same Region share access to its sites, but each town receives
its own output and holds its own resources. Two towns with the same owner do
not automatically share a stockpile, even in the same Region. Deliberate
shipments can move goods between owned towns; transport details remain open.

Under `DEC-022`, each town assigns its population to timber and specialist
production using separate worker sliders. Both assignments use the same town
population: a citizen cannot occupy two jobs, and the combined assignments
cannot exceed that population. Each slider is also limited by the applicable
production-site maximum. When editing an assignment, its available population
includes its existing workers and unassigned citizens, not workers committed
to the other site.

Unassigned citizens provide hourly town income. More resource workers therefore
leave fewer citizens generating that income; exact production and income rates
will be decided later. Reading the Founder's example as 1,000 people gives
250 timber workers, 250 specialist workers and 500 income-producing citizens.
These figures are illustrative, not starting values. With 100 available people
and a site maximum of 100, assigning all 100 there leaves none for the other
site or this unassigned-citizen income.

Under `DEC-023`, players contribute timber towards expanding the shared regional
sites. This applies to both timber and specialist production sites. A site
upgrade raises the number of workers it supports; its limit is not a permanent
fixed allowance. Towns using that site can benefit from the higher capacity,
but must still have enough available people to assign. Expansion neither
creates population nor automatically fills the extra jobs. Higher production
per worker is not an approved consequence.

Under `DEC-024`, the action is named "Contribute Timber", replacing "Donate".
"Site expansion" remains proposed heading wording.

Exact timber requirements, capacities by level, contribution handling and
upgrade timing remain open. Whether the site's maximum applies separately to
each town or across all towns is a separate question. Production and income
rates, currency, upkeep, other population roles and falling-population handling
also remain undecided.

The Founder's screenshots show three distinct reference views: a world of
islands, an island with town locations and resource sites, and the interior of
a town. DEC-047 initially followed that navigation hierarchy; DEC-057 now
changes the original AZMARA preview to World → Region → Town with continental
desert geography. DEC-051's 16 ordinary positions plus one premium preview
remain in the current Region layout, separate from the town's 23 building plots. Geography variants,
ownership, colonisation and premium access/cost rules remain open.
Reference religious features and artwork are not adopted.

## Cultural and historical care

Treat the setting's real religious history and its sources respectfully. The principal focus
is civilisation, cities, economics, scholarship, exploration, diplomacy,
technology, trade and strategy.

Do not turn important religious concepts, sacred figures, sacred texts or acts
of worship into cheap or trivialised game mechanics. A civilisation theme is
not permission to use sacred material as a power-up or commercial gimmick.

When proposing real terminology, architecture, institutions, locations or
events, research them using reliable sources. Distinguish sourced historical
information from uncertainty, fiction and deliberate alternative history.
Do not present invented details or convenient anachronisms as historical fact.

The existing `DEC-007` exclusion of gods, polytheism, miracles and divine
powers remains unchanged. The new setting does not introduce worship-based
bonuses or substitute sacred concepts for the excluded deity mechanics.

The later explicit `DEC-048` exception retains a Prayer Area instead of the
reference Temple, with a possible civic satisfaction benefit to design later.
It is a respectful quiet community space, without sacred figures/text or prayer
sold as a power-up. Chronos’ Forge remains a working name for secular timekeeping,
construction planning and fabrication. No gods, divine powers or miracles are
introduced; neither proposed benefit has numerical rules or gameplay yet.

## Original identity and implementation

Ikariam is a reference for the broad appeal of persistent civilisation-building
games, not a blueprint to reproduce. The originality requirement in `DEC-002`
remains unchanged.

Our world, gameplay systems, economy, progression, map, interface, art
direction, buildings, resources, units, research, diplomacy, multiplayer
systems, lore, balance and code must develop their own identity. A change of
names or visual theme is not sufficient. Do not copy proprietary code, assets,
text, maps, balancing or game datasets from the reference. `DEC-013` permits
the supplied reference's first-draft screen arrangement, not imported artwork
or a permanent cloned identity.
